## Camera (21)

- **High Angle Shot** — Camera positioned above the subject, looking down. Provides a unique perspective and can make the subject appear smaller within their environment. (`p/high-angle-shot`)
- **Low Angle Shot** — Camera positioned below the subject, looking up. Makes the subject appear powerful, heroic, or imposing. (`p/low-angle-shot`)
- **Dutch Angle** — Camera tilted on its roll axis, creating a diagonal horizon line. Creates visual tension, unease, or psychological tension. (`p/dutch-angle`)
- **Bird's Eye View** — Camera positioned directly overhead looking straight down at 90 degrees. Top-down perspective used for reveals and geometric compositions. (`p/bird-s-eye-view`)
- **Extreme Close-Up** — Tight framing on a specific detail — eyes, lips, hands. Creates intimacy and emphasizes emotion. (`p/extreme-close-up`)
- **Long Shot** — Shows the subject's full body within their environment. Establishes spatial relationships and character within their world. (`p/long-shot`)
- **Medium Shot** — Frames the subject from roughly the waist up. Balances facial expression with body language. (`p/medium-shot`)
- **Close-Up** — Tight framing on the subject's face. Captures subtle emotions and creates intimacy with the audience. (`p/close-up`)
- **Worm's Eye View** — Camera at ground level pointing straight up. Creates dramatic vertigo effect and emphasizes scale. (`p/worm-s-eye-view`)
- **Over the Shoulder Shot** — Camera positioned behind one character's shoulder, showing what they see. Creates depth and perspective. (`p/over-the-shoulder-shot`)
- **Extreme Wide Shot** — Very wide framing showing vast environment with subject very small. Establishes scale and place. (`p/extreme-wide-shot`)
- **Back Shot** — Camera behind the subject showing what they see. Creates mystery and invites the viewer into their perspective. (`p/back-shot`)
- **POV Shot** — Point of view shot — what the character sees through their own eyes. Immersive first-person perspective. (`p/pov-shot`)
- **Establishing Shot** — Wide opening shot that introduces a location and sets the scene before more intimate framing follows. (`p/establishing-shot`)
- **Aerial Shot** — Drone or helicopter perspective — high above the scene, revealing scale and spatial relationships from the sky. (`p/aerial-shot`)
- **Medium Close-Up** — Frames the subject from chest to top of head — between a close-up and medium shot. The dialogue workhorse. (`p/medium-close-up`)
- **Cowboy Shot** — Frames the subject from mid-thigh to top of head — the iconic Western framing that makes characters feel grounded and ready. (`p/cowboy-shot`)
- **Eye-Level Shot** — The neutral baseline angle — camera at exactly the subject's eye height, lens horizontal. Feels natural and direct. (`p/eye-level-shot`)
- **Three-Quarter View** — Subject's body turned 45 degrees from camera while face is angled back. The most common portrait pose. (`p/three-quarter-view`)
- **Two-Shot** — Two subjects framed together in the same shot. The standard framing for dialogue and relationship scenes. (`p/two-shot`)
- **Profile Shot** — Subject facing exactly sideways — pure silhouette of the face in profile. Strong graphic portrait style. (`p/profile-shot`)

## Lighting (28)

- **Chiaroscuro Lighting** — Extreme contrast between light and dark, inspired by Renaissance painters like Caravaggio. Creates dramatic, painterly atmosphere. (`p/chiaroscuro-lighting`)
- **Rim Lighting** — Light source positioned behind the subject creates a bright outline around their silhouette. Separates subject from background dramatically. (`p/rim-lighting`)
- **Silhouette** — Subject appears as a dark shape against a bright background. Eliminates detail, emphasizes form and posture. (`p/silhouette`)
- **Golden Hour** — Warm soft directional light during the hour after sunrise or before sunset. Creates warm, romantic, nostalgic atmosphere. (`p/golden-hour`)
- **Volumetric Lighting** — Visible beams of light cutting through fog, mist, or haze. Creates depth, atmosphere, and a sense of the divine. (`p/volumetric-lighting`)
- **Split Lighting** — Light illuminates exactly half the face while the other half is in shadow. Creates duality, mystery, hidden nature. (`p/split-lighting`)
- **Practical Lighting** — Light sources visible within the frame — lanterns, lamps, candles. Creates naturalistic, immersive lighting. (`p/practical-lighting`)
- **Butterfly Lighting** — Key light positioned above and in front of the subject, creating a butterfly-shaped shadow under the nose. Classic glamour portrait style. (`p/butterfly-lighting`)
- **Loop Lighting** — Key light at 45 degrees creates a small loop-shaped shadow from the nose onto the cheek. The most common portrait lighting. (`p/loop-lighting`)
- **Rembrandt Lighting** — Defined by a small illuminated triangle of light on the shadow side of the face, just below the eye. Dramatic painterly portrait lighting. (`p/rembrandt-lighting`)
- **High Key Lighting** — Bright, even lighting with minimal shadows. Creates upbeat, cheerful, optimistic mood. (`p/high-key-lighting`)
- **Low Key Lighting** — Dramatic lighting with deep shadows and high contrast. Creates moody, mysterious, or noir atmosphere. (`p/low-key-lighting`)
- **Blue Hour** — Period just after sunset or before sunrise when the sky takes on a deep blue color. Cool, magical, contemplative. (`p/blue-hour`)
- **Neon Color Pop** — Vivid colored lighting (neon, gels) creates strong color contrast and modern aesthetic. (`p/neon-color-pop`)
- **Back Lit With Flare** — Sun directly behind subject creates lens flares, hair glow, and dreamy bokeh. (`p/back-lit-with-flare`)
- **Broad Lighting** — Light falls on the side of the face that's turned toward the camera. Makes the face appear wider and more open. (`p/broad-lighting`)
- **Short Lighting** — Light falls on the side of the face turned away from the camera. Creates more shadows, slimming and dramatic effect. (`p/short-lighting`)
- **Hard Light** — A direct, undiffused light source that creates sharp, high-contrast shadows. Dramatic and edgy. (`p/hard-light`)
- **Soft Light** — A diffused, gentle light source that wraps around the subject. Flattering and natural-looking. (`p/soft-light`)
- **Day for Night** — A classic film technique — shooting in daytime but color-grading to look like night. Iconic in Westerns and old cinema. (`p/day-for-night`)
- **Dappled Light** — Natural light filtered through leaves or branches, creating spots of light and shadow on the subject. Magical and organic. (`p/dappled-light`)
- **Uplighting** — Light shining upward at the subject, creating an unnatural, theatrical, sometimes sinister look. (`p/uplighting`)
- **Three-Point Lighting** — Classic lighting setup — key, fill, and back light working together. The foundation of cinema and studio portraiture. (`p/three-point-lighting`)
- **Natural Light** — Pure available daylight, no artificial sources. The most natural, candid, documentary look. (`p/natural-light`)
- **Side Lighting** — Light source positioned at 90 degrees to the side of the subject. Half the face lit, half in gradient shadow. (`p/side-lighting`)
- **Front Lighting** — Light coming from the direction of the camera. Flat, even, no shadows — the safe commercial look. (`p/front-lighting`)
- **Top Lighting (Hatchet Light)** — Hard light from directly overhead. Creates deep eye-socket shadows and dramatic noir mood. (`p/top-lighting-hatchet-light`)
- **Catchlight** — Bright reflection of the light source visible in the subject's eyes. The hallmark of lively portraits. (`p/catchlight`)

## Lens (13)

- **Shallow Depth of Field** — Only the subject is in sharp focus while foreground and background blur into bokeh. Isolates the subject cinematically. (`p/shallow-depth-of-field`)
- **Deep Focus** — Everything from foreground to background is in sharp focus. Shows the subject and environment with equal clarity. (`p/deep-focus`)
- **Rack Focus** — Focus shifts from one subject to another within the same shot. Directs audience attention. (`p/rack-focus`)
- **Lens Flare** — Light scattering within the lens creates streaks, circles, or halos. Adds atmospheric realism and warmth. (`p/lens-flare`)
- **Tilt-Shift** — Selective focus that makes real scenes look like miniature dioramas. (`p/tilt-shift`)
- **Split Diopter** — Special lens that keeps two different distances in focus simultaneously, creating an unusual visual effect. (`p/split-diopter`)
- **Wide Angle Lens** — Wide field of view captures expansive scenes. Slight distortion at edges. Creates immersive, environmental feel. (`p/wide-angle-lens`)
- **Telephoto Lens** — Long focal length compresses background, makes subject prominent. Classic portrait look. (`p/telephoto-lens`)
- **Fisheye Lens** — Extreme wide angle lens that creates spherical distortion. Curved horizons, exaggerated perspective. (`p/fisheye-lens`)
- **Macro Detail** — Extreme close-up of small details. Reveals textures and intimacy invisible to the naked eye. (`p/macro-detail`)
- **Vignette** — Darkened or softened edges drawing the eye toward the center. Creates focus and vintage feel. (`p/vignette`)
- **Anamorphic Bokeh** — Distinctive oval-shaped out-of-focus lights from anamorphic cinema lenses. Cinematic Hollywood look. (`p/anamorphic-bokeh`)
- **Film Grain** — The textured noise pattern of analog film stock. Adds organic, vintage, cinematic feel to digital images. (`p/film-grain`)

## Composition (20)

- **Symmetrical Composition** — Subject centered with mirrored elements on both sides. Creates balance, order, and a sense of importance. (`p/symmetrical-composition`)
- **Rule of Thirds** — Subject placed at one of the intersections of a 3x3 grid. Creates dynamic, naturally pleasing compositions. (`p/rule-of-thirds`)
- **Leading Lines** — Lines within the scene guide the viewer's eye toward the subject — paths, architecture, natural forms. (`p/leading-lines`)
- **Frame Within a Frame** — Using elements in the scene — doorways, windows, arches — to create a secondary frame around the subject. (`p/frame-within-a-frame`)
- **Negative Space** — Large areas of empty space surrounding the subject. Creates feelings of contemplation, peace, or solitude. (`p/negative-space`)
- **Foreground Framing** — Using foreground elements to add depth and frame the subject. Creates layered, three-dimensional compositions. (`p/foreground-framing`)
- **Diagonal Composition** — Composition built around strong diagonal lines running across the frame. Creates movement, energy, and dynamic tension. (`p/diagonal-composition`)
- **Triangle Composition** — Three visual anchor points arranged in a triangular shape. Creates stability, balance, and visual hierarchy. (`p/triangle-composition`)
- **S-Curve Composition** — Subtle S-shaped curve guides the eye through the frame. Elegant and graceful. (`p/s-curve-composition`)
- **Central Composition** — Subject placed exactly in the center of the frame. Creates impact, focus, and balance. (`p/central-composition`)
- **Low Horizon Line** — Horizon placed in lower third, emphasizing sky or vertical elements above. (`p/low-horizon-line`)
- **High Horizon Line** — Horizon placed high in the frame, emphasizing the foreground. Creates intimacy with ground-level detail. (`p/high-horizon-line`)
- **Minimalism** — Stripping the composition down to the absolute essentials. One subject, vast empty space, silence. (`p/minimalism`)
- **Fill the Frame** — Subject completely fills the frame with no negative space. Creates intimacy and detail focus. (`p/fill-the-frame`)
- **Repetition & Pattern** — Repeating elements create visual rhythm — when the pattern is the subject. Often broken by one element for emphasis. (`p/repetition-pattern`)
- **Forced Perspective** — Optical illusion playing with scale — making distant objects appear to interact with closer ones. Iconic in fantasy films. (`p/forced-perspective`)
- **Golden Ratio** — Composition based on the Fibonacci spiral and the 1:1.618 divine proportion. Creates naturally pleasing, balanced arrangements. (`p/golden-ratio`)
- **Headroom** — Proper empty space above the subject's head. A classical composition rule for balanced framing. (`p/headroom`)
- **Lead Room (Nose Room)** — Empty space placed in the direction the subject is looking or moving. Allows the gaze to 'breathe'. (`p/lead-room-nose-room`)
- **Layering & Depth** — Foreground, midground, and background layers all visible. Creates rich three-dimensional depth in a flat image. (`p/layering-depth`)

## Color (6)

- **Monochrome (Black & White)** — Black and white photography removes color, emphasizing form, light, and emotion. (`p/monochrome-black-white`)
- **Sepia Tone** — Warm brown tones that evoke nostalgia, history, and antique photography. (`p/sepia-tone`)
- **Teal & Orange Grade** — Hollywood-style color grading: shadows pushed to teal, highlights to orange. Creates cinematic contrast. (`p/teal-orange-grade`)
- **Complementary Colors** — Colors opposite on the color wheel create maximum visual impact. Red/green, blue/orange, purple/yellow. (`p/complementary-colors`)
- **Desaturated Color Grade** — Reduced color saturation creates a muted, melancholic, or dreamy atmosphere. (`p/desaturated-color-grade`)
- **Vibrant Saturation** — Boosted saturation creates vivid, energetic, joyful imagery. Pop and energy. (`p/vibrant-saturation`)

## Creative (8)

- **Double Exposure** — Two images overlaid into one, creating a dreamlike or symbolic composition. Often combines a portrait with nature or a scene. (`p/double-exposure`)
- **Reflection Composition** — Using reflective surfaces — water, mirrors, glass — to create symmetry, duality, or visual interest. (`p/reflection-composition`)
- **Motion Blur** — Long exposure capturing movement as blur while subject remains sharp. Creates dynamic energy. (`p/motion-blur`)
- **Long Exposure** — Long shutter speed captures movement as silky blur while static elements remain sharp. (`p/long-exposure`)
- **Smoke / Mist Atmosphere** — Atmospheric smoke or mist adds depth, mystery, and a dreamlike quality. (`p/smoke-mist-atmosphere`)
- **Bokeh Portrait** — Heavy out-of-focus background creates beautiful soft circles of light around the subject. (`p/bokeh-portrait`)
- **Light Cracks (Kintsugi)** — Beams of light cutting through architectural gaps create dramatic golden lines, like kintsugi (golden cracks). (`p/light-cracks-kintsugi`)
- **Wet Plate Look** — The look of antique wet-collodion photography — dreamy, slightly distorted, with vignetting and rich tonal range. (`p/wet-plate-look`)

## Movement (17)

- **Push In (Dolly In)** — Camera slowly moves toward the subject, creating intimacy and intensifying focus on emotion or revelation. (`p/push-in-dolly-in`)
- **Pull Out (Dolly Out)** — Camera slowly moves away from the subject, revealing the environment and creating a sense of isolation or scale. (`p/pull-out-dolly-out`)
- **Pan Left** — Camera rotates horizontally on its axis, sweeping the view left. Reveals environment or follows action. (`p/pan-left`)
- **Tilt Up** — Camera rotates vertically upward. Reveals scale, height, or surprise. (`p/tilt-up`)
- **Tilt Down** — Camera rotates vertically downward. Often used for dramatic reveals. (`p/tilt-down`)
- **Side Tracking Shot** — Camera moves alongside the subject as they walk, maintaining the same distance. Creates a sense of journey. (`p/side-tracking-shot`)
- **Forward Tracking** — Camera follows behind the subject as they walk forward. Immersive POV-adjacent experience. (`p/forward-tracking`)
- **Orbit Shot** — Camera circles around the subject, who remains the focal point. Creates dynamic, dramatic emphasis. (`p/orbit-shot`)
- **Crane Up (Boom Up)** — Camera rises vertically, revealing more of the environment from above. Often used for dramatic reveals. (`p/crane-up-boom-up`)
- **Dolly Zoom (Vertigo Effect)** — Camera moves toward subject while zooming out (or vice versa). Background appears to stretch dramatically while subject stays the same size. Creates psychological disorientation. (`p/dolly-zoom-vertigo-effect`)
- **Whip Pan** — Extremely fast horizontal pan that creates a motion blur transition. Energetic and dynamic. (`p/whip-pan`)
- **Handheld Shot** — Camera held by hand, creating natural shake and movement. Adds urgency, realism, or intimacy. (`p/handheld-shot`)
- **Steadicam Glide** — Smooth flowing camera movement that follows the subject without shake. Cinematic and professional. (`p/steadicam-glide`)
- **Rack Focus (Video)** — Focus shifts from one element to another within the same shot, redirecting viewer attention. (`p/rack-focus-video`)
- **Zoom In** — Lens zooms toward subject while camera stays still. Different feel than dolly-in — flatter, more clinical. (`p/zoom-in`)
- **Zoom Out** — Lens zooms away from subject while camera stays still. Reveals environment. (`p/zoom-out`)
- **Vertical Pan** — Vertical sweep showing height and verticality of a scene. (`p/vertical-pan`)

## Time (1)

- **Slow Motion** — Footage played back slower than real-time. Emphasizes beauty, drama, or emotion of a moment. (`p/slow-motion`)

# Expansions (reference)

What each official expansion adds, key numbers, and how they combine. Sources: catan.com rulebook index, Wikipedia expansion articles (Seafarers, Cities & Knights, Traders & Barbarians, List of Catan products).

## The four core expansions
| Expansion | Year | VP target | One-line identity |
|---|---|---|---|
| Seafarers | 1997 | 10 (most scenarios 12) | Ships, multi-island scenarios, gold hexes, the pirate |
| Cities & Knights | 1998/99 | **13** | Commodities, city improvements, knights, barbarian fleet |
| Traders & Barbarians | 2008 | varies by scenario | A toolbox of modules: 2-player, events, camels, fishermen, rivers |
| Explorers & Pirates | 2013 | varies | 5 scenarios: sea exploration, missions, pirate fighting, fish/spice |

## Seafarers — key mechanics
- **Ships** = roads on water: 1 wood + 1 wool; a chain must be anchored by a settlement at its start; an unanchored end may relocate 1 ship/turn (not one just placed).
- **Gold fields**: adjacent settlement takes 1 ANY resource (city: 2). Balanced by low-value tokens (2/3 pips) or remoteness. Gold buys resources only — never commodities (combined with C&K).
- **The Pirate**: waterborne robber; on a 7 or a knight you move robber OR pirate; it shakes down nearby ships and blocks new ship building near it (doesn't stop settlements/harbors).
- Longest Road becomes **Longest Trade Route** (roads + ships count; a settlement is required between a road and a ship segment for continuity).
- Scenarios: *Heading to New Shores* (intro), *The Four Islands* (bonus VP per new island), *The Fog Island* (tile-back exploration; discovering land pays a resource), *Through the Desert* (cross a desert wall or go by sea for island VPs), plus newer *The Forgotten Tribe*, *Cloth for Catan*, *The Pirate Island*, *The Wonders of Catan*.
- Edition caution: old (pre-5th) Seafarers maps don't fit the newer frames (frame shape changed; separate frame add-on exists).

## Cities & Knights — key mechanics (the heavy one)
- Plays to **13 VP**; development cards are replaced by **progress cards**; "Largest Army" removed; adds a 3rd **event die**; building-cost cards replaced by the City Upgrade Calendar.
- **Commodities**: cities on wool/wood/ore hexes produce 1 resource + 1 commodity (cloth from pasture, paper from forest, coin from mountain; 36 cards, 12 each). Grain/brick cities still double-produce resources; grain now ACTIVATES knights, brick builds city walls.
- **City improvements** — three commodity tracks, five levels each: **Science** (green; paper, from forests), **Trade** (yellow; cloth, from pastures), **Politics** (blue; coins, from mountains). Level III abilities: Science = if a roll produces nothing for you, take 1 resource of your choice; Trade = exchange 2 identical commodities for any 1 resource or commodity; Politics = promote knights to mighty (strength 3). First to level IV claims that track's **Metropolis** (that city = 4 VP; stealable by out-leveling; one metropolis per track).
- **Knights**: 24 physical units (6/color; +12 in the 5-6 extension). Buy = 1 wool + 1 ore; upgrade rank = another 1 wool + 1 ore (weak → strong → mighty; mighty only via Politics III). **Activating** a knight each turn costs 1 grain and deactivates it after acting; knights cannot act the turn they're bought/placed. Active knights move along roads/ships, displace lower-rank enemy knights, and dispel the robber/pirate; knights also block roads like buildings. Brick builds **city walls** (one-time defense per city vs. barbarian raids).
- **Barbarian fleet**: event-die ship faces advance the fleet; on arrival, attack strength = all players' cities+metropolises vs. total levels of ACTIVE knights; if barbarians win, the weakest defender's city downgrades to a settlement. City-gate faces grant progress cards. Metropolis holders get production bonuses.
- Much longer (~90-120 min), highest interaction; "the chess expansion" of the family.

## Traders & Barbarians — modules (pick-and-mix; each needs base game)
- **Catan for Two** — the official 2-player variant (each player controls 2 colors; second color trades at worse rates, moves robber, blocks).
- **The Fishermen of Catan** — fishing fleet; fish as a flexible buffer resource.
- **The Rivers of Catan** — gold from controlling longest river; pay gold to use opponents' roads.
- **Traders & Barbarians (flagship)** — wagons + caravans deliver trade-hex commodities for VP/gold; barbarians roam paths; baggage-train upgrades.
- **Barbarian Attack** — knights from a castle repel coastal-hex conquests (3 barbarians on a hex = conquered, stops producing; capture them for prisoner-VP pairs).
- **Catan Event Cards** — a 36-card deck replaces dice: production equals the 2d6 distribution but without variance storms; removes the discard rule. Later re-released inside T&B.
- Plus **The Horse Overlords**-era camel scenarios (Treasures, Dragons & Adventurers pack, EN 2021).

## Explorers & Pirates (2013)
Five scenarios / three missions: *Land Ho*, *Pirate Lairs*, *Fish for Catan*, *Spices for Catan*, *Explorers & Pirates*. Adds face-down exploration hexes, sea combat vs. pirates (convert ships to warships via knights), fish & spice as new resources, colony missions (build a 3-VP colony: 2 settlements + city on a new island), and multi-stage mission cards.

## Small/boxed add-ons
- **5–6 Player Extensions** — one per core game (base, Seafarers, C&K, T&B); 5–6 with an expansion needs BOTH extensions.
- **Oil Springs** (2011; originally free download) — oil fields power extra production & metropolises; overuse triggers disasters; sequester oil for VP. Climate allegory; included in **New Energies** (2022) which adds a green-upgrades module.
- **Frenemies / Helpers of Catan** — favor tokens reward gifting, joined roads, and neutralizing the robber.
- **Historical Scenarios I (Alexander & Cheops, 1998) / II (Troy & Great Wall, 2001)** — scenario packs requiring base components.

## Compatibility cheat-sheet
- Base + Seafarers + C&K + T&B modules combine via official combo rules published on catan.com (e.g. "New Shores," "Seafarers × C&K," "Barbarian Attack × T&B").
- General rules of thumb: C&K replaces dev cards wholesale (can't mix decks); C&K's manual advises against mixing with exploration scenarios; Seafarers' pirate coexists with C&K knights by written combo rules.
- Match editions: 4th-ed expansions don't align with 5th/6th frames; 6th Ed English re-releases shipped 2025 (Seafarers, C&K rulebooks CN3083+).

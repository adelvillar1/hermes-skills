# Strategy & Tactics (reference)

Section TACTICS from the official 2020 Almanac (canonical advice, paraphrased) + established competitive heuristics that follow from the rules and math. Sources: catan.com Almanac; CATAN tournament practice.

## The official Almanac bullets
1. **Brick and lumber dominate the opening** — you need both for roads + first settlements; place at least one of your first settlements on a good forest or hills hex.
2. **Don't underestimate harbors** — a player with productive fields should work toward the wheat 2:1 port.
3. **Leave room to expand** — read opponents' sites and roads before placing; beware getting surrounded; a central starting settlement can be cut off from the coast.
4. **The more you trade, the better your chances** — offer trades even when it's not your turn (you may trade during another player's turn if they deal with you).

## Opening placement (highest-leverage decision in Catan)
Score a candidate intersection on five axes:
(a) total pips of its hexes; (b) resource diversity — ore + wheat unlock the city/dev engine fastest; (c) expansion: ≥2 open road directions; (d) anti-robber: avoid depending on two settlements that share both hexes; (e) port angle — is a 2:1 reachable in 1–2 roads?
- The reversed second round means the LAST first-pick gets the BEST second-pick — if you pick first, take the spot whose value is robust; if you pick last, you can double-dip or execute a block.
- A ~12-pip cage beats an ~18-pip cornered spot with no escape; the 15-road cap is real.
- The desert-adjacent corner: only for a specific high-number pair; it hands the robber a free "parking" move.

## Engine vs. points
- City-first (2 wheat + 3 ore) permanently doubles that production point — the default plan whenever ore flows.
- In 4-player races, extra settlements (+1 VP, +production, +board presence) often beat the first city; in 3-player, the engine usually wins.
- Roads score nothing by themselves — build toward something (port, expansion, Longest Route steal). 15 roads is the ceiling.
- Dev cards pay when building space is closed or you need robber tempo; 56% knights = investment in pressure. Stop buying when the VP-card odds thin out or you're within 2 of 10 (build points directly).

## Trading strategy
- Trade your abundance for your bottleneck; price it like a merchant: a card an opponent can 4:1 anyway is nearly free to them — charge scarcity, not effort.
- Deny the leader: a trade that costs you 1 VP tempo but hands the leader 2 VP is net-negative for your win probability. The table polices itself; the player who never blocks loses to the one who blocks at the right time.
- Never say "I can't help you" while showing your hand; keep information hidden (hand counts are public at production, choices are not).

## Blocking & the robber
- A legal settlement on a choke intersection splits Longest Routes and seals expansion — the Distance Rule itself is a weapon: settle near a hex to deny a corner.
- Robber target order: leader's most productive hex you don't share → whoever holds the card you need → repeat-victim sparingly (they'll retaliate and freeze trade).
- Moving the robber to the desert is a real (underused) option: avoids making an enemy when you're already safe.

## Common beginner mistakes
- 4:1 banking while a player would trade 1:1 — bank trade is the last resort.
- Placing both starts toward each other (self-clustering).
- Hoarding >7 cards through a live board.
- Forgetting you may play a dev card BEFORE rolling; forgetting a Knight moves AND steals; revealing VP cards early.
- Ignoring the low-probability 2/12 spots: a 2+12+11 ore/brick/wood triangle is a legitimately competitive line — rare but robber-proof production.

## Coaching mental model
Track three ledgers: **income** (pips owned), **race** (known VP on board vs. what's plausible in hands), **chokepoints** (resources only you can supply). Most "who's winning?" disputes come from players watching only the first ledger.

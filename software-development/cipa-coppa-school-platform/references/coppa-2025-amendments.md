# COPPA Rule — 2025 Amendments and the Ed-Tech Gap

Distilled from the final rule: 90 FR 16918 (Apr. 22, 2025), FTC, RIN 3084-AB20,
FTC Docket FTC-2024-0003, amending 16 CFR Part 312. 66 pages.

## Dates — the two that matter

| Event | Date |
|---|---|
| Published in Federal Register | Apr. 22, 2025 |
| **Effective date** | **June 23, 2025** |
| **Compliance date** | **April 22, 2026** |
| Carve-out from the compliance date | §312.11(d)(1), (d)(4), and (g) |

Exact rule text: "Except with respect to § 312.11(d)(1), (d)(4), and (g),
regulated entities have until April 22, 2026 to comply."

The one-year gap between effective and compliance date is why pre-2025 and
post-2025 summaries of Part 312 disagree. Always cite which version you mean.

## What the amendments actually changed

Per the FTC's own summary, the final amendments:

| Change | Detail |
|---|---|
| **New definition** | Mixed audience website or online service — clarifies an existing sub-category of child-directed services |
| **Online contact information** | Expanded to include **mobile telephone numbers** |
| **Personal information** | Expanded to include **government-issued identifiers** and **biometric identifiers** usable for automated/semi-automated recognition |
| **Support for internal operations** | Clarified that info collected for the enumerated activities may be used or disclosed to carry out those activities |
| **Website directed to children** | Added examples of evidence the Commission may consider on audience composition and intended audience; paragraph (3) aligned with the new mixed-audience definition |
| **Notices** | Modified operators' obligations for direct and online notices |
| **Security / deletion / retention** | Modified obligations for information security, deletion, and retention protocols |
| **Safe harbor** | New annual assessment, disclosure, and reporting requirements for FTC-approved COPPA Safe Harbor programs |
| **Parental consent** | Amendments to consent requirements, **methods** of obtaining verifiable parental consent, and the consent **exceptions** |
| **Style** | "web site" → "website" throughout; minor grammatical changes from the 2024 NPRM |

## §312.8 — security program (2025 text)

The baseline duty: establish and maintain **reasonable procedures** to protect
the confidentiality, security, and integrity of personal information collected
from children, and take reasonable steps to release children's PI only to
service providers and third parties capable of maintaining those protections.

The 2025 rule adds the requirement to establish, implement, and maintain a
**written children's personal information security program** containing
safeguards appropriate to:

- the sensitivity of the PI collected from children, and
- the operator's size, complexity, and nature and scope of activities.

To establish and maintain that program, an operator must:

- designate one or more **employees to coordinate** the program;
- conduct **assessments** to identify internal and external risks to
  confidentiality, security, and integrity, and the sufficiency of existing
  safeguards;
- design, implement, and maintain **safeguards** controlling the identified
  risks;
- **regularly test and monitor** the effectiveness of those safeguards; and
- **evaluate and modify** the program **at least annually**.

**§312.8(c) third-party assurance:** before allowing other operators, service
providers, or third parties to collect or maintain children's PI on the
operator's behalf, or before releasing children's PI to them, the operator
must take reasonable steps to determine they are capable of maintaining
confidentiality, security, and integrity, and must obtain **written
assurances** that they will employ reasonable measures to do so.

## §312.10 — retention and deletion (2025 text)

This is the section with the sharpest new teeth. Operators shall:

- retain PI collected online from a child **only as long as reasonably
  necessary** to fulfill the specific purpose(s) for which it was collected;
- when no longer reasonably necessary, **delete it using reasonable measures**
  to protect against unauthorized access to, or use of, the information in
  connection with its deletion;
- **personal information may not be retained indefinitely** — stated outright;
- at minimum, establish, implement, and maintain a **written data retention
  policy** setting forth (i) the purposes for which children's PI is
  collected, (ii) the **business need** for retaining it, and (iii) a
  **timeframe for deletion**; and
- provide that written retention policy **in the online notice** required by
  §312.4(d).

The FTC's stated rationale: these revisions prevent retention beyond the
specific documented purposes, ensure parents receive notice of intended use
**and a hyperlink to the online notice** before consenting, and implement
baseline **data minimization** requirements that reduce the burden on parents.

## §312.4 — notice content (2025 text)

The online notice must describe what the operator collects from children,
including whether the service enables a child to make PI **publicly
available**; how it uses such information; and its disclosure practices,
including **the identities and specific categories of any third parties** to
which it discloses PI and the purposes for such disclosures — plus **the
operator's data retention policy as required under §312.10**.

Additional required items:

- **(3)** If applicable, the specific internal operations for which a
  persistent identifier was collected under §312.5(c)(7), and the means used
  to ensure it is not used to contact a specific individual (including
  behavioral advertising), to amass a profile, or for any other purpose
  beyond supporting internal operations.
- **(4)** For audio files of a child's voice under §312.5(c)(9): how they are
  used, and that they are **deleted immediately** after responding to the
  request.
- **(5)** If applicable, that the parent can **review or have deleted** the
  child's PI and refuse further collection or use, and the procedures for
  doing so.

## The ed-tech gap — the most consequential non-change

The 2024 NPRM proposed a set of ed-tech amendments:

- new definitions of **School** and **School-authorized education purpose**,
- provisions governing **collection of information from children in schools**,
- codifying a **school authorization exception** to verifiable parental
  consent, and
- changes to §312.4(b) and to the heading of §312.4(c).

**The FTC did not finalize any of it.**

Stated reason: in Fall 2024 the U.S. Department of Education affirmed its
intention to propose amendments to the FERPA regulations at 34 CFR 99, to
update and ask-the-user prompt provisions governing non-consensual disclosures of PII from
education records to third parties. To avoid amendments that might **conflict
with potential amendments to DOE's FERPA regulations**, the Commission declined
to finalize the ed-tech and school-role provisions "at this time."

The FTC added: it **will continue to enforce COPPA in the ed tech context
consistent with its existing guidance.**

### What this means in practice

- The school-authorization exception remains **guidance**, not codified text.
  It can be relied on in the ordinary case, but it is not a regulatory safe
  harbor with defined terms.
- "School" and "school-authorized education purpose" have **no regulatory
  definition** in Part 312. Read the 2024 NPRM's proposed definitions for a
  sense of the FTC's thinking; they are not binding.
- The comment record shows live pressure to tighten this area regardless —
  commenters asked the FTC to require vendors relying on school authorization
  to implement enhanced protections (encryption at rest and in motion, regular
  independent audits, audit results to parents on request, breach
  notification to schools and parents). The FTC noted these and declined for
  now. Read as directional: **expect this to come back.**
- Related context worth knowing: the FTC issued a **COPPA policy statement on
  age verification technologies** (Feb. 25, 2026), indicating continued
  rulemaking attention in this space.

## Anti-patterns

- **Citing the 2025 amendments as settling ed tech.** The opposite — the FTC
  explicitly punted to avoid colliding with DOE rulemaking.
- **Using pre-2025 retention or security language.** §312.10's written-policy
  and no-indefinite-retention requirements, and §312.8's written security
  program, are new operative text.
- **Assuming the compliance date is when you should start.** For a platform
  that turns out to be in scope, the written security program (annual review,
  designated coordinator, documented risk assessments) and written retention
  policy are the long-lead items.

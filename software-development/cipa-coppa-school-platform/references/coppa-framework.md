# COPPA Framework — Structure of the Regime

Distilled from the COPPA statute (15 U.S.C. 6501-6506), the COPPA Rule at
16 CFR Part 312 as amended in 2025 (90 FR 16918, Apr. 22, 2025), and FTC
ed-tech guidance. Structured notes, not a reproduction of the rule text.

## The two-layer structure

| Layer | What it is | Cite |
|---|---|---|
| Statute | Children's Online Privacy Protection Act of 1998, enacted 1998 | 15 U.S.C. 6501-6505 |
| Rule | COPPA Rule, effective Apr. 21, 2000; amended 2013 and 2025 | 16 CFR Part 312 |
| Guidance | FTC statements on ed tech, school authorization, age verification | FTC business guidance |

The Rule is what you actually comply with. The statute is short; Part 312 is
where the operational detail lives.

## Part 312 section map

| § | Title | Substance |
|---|---|---|
| 312.1 | Scope | Coverage of the Rule |
| 312.2 | Definitions | The definitions that decide everything — see below |
| 312.3 | Regulation of unfair/deceptive acts | The five core operator duties, spelled out |
| 312.4 | Notice | Online notice + direct notice to parents |
| 312.5 | Parental consent | Verifiable parental consent and its exceptions |
| 312.6 | Parental review | Parent's right to review and refuse further use |
| 312.7 | No conditioning participation | Can't gate an activity on excess disclosure |
| 312.8 | Confidentiality, security, integrity | Security program; third-party assurances |
| 312.9 | Enforcement | Enforcement mechanics |
| 312.10 | Data retention and deletion | Written retention policy; no indefinite retention |
| 312.11 | Safe harbor | FTC-approved self-regulatory programs |

## §312.3 — the five core duties

An operator of a child-directed service, or one with actual knowledge it is
collecting/maintaining PI from a child, must:

- **(a)** Provide notice on the site/service of what it collects from children,
  how it uses it, and its disclosure practices (§312.4(b)).
- **(b)** Obtain **verifiable parental consent** before any collection, use,
  and/or disclosure of PI from children (§312.5).
- **(c)** Give parents a reasonable means to review the PI collected from their
  child and to refuse further use or maintenance (§312.6).
- **(d)** Not condition a child's participation in a game, prize, or other
  activity on disclosing more PI than is reasonably necessary (§312.7).
- **(e)** Establish and maintain **reasonable procedures** to protect the
  confidentiality, security, and integrity of PI collected from children
  (§312.8).

## §312.2 — the definitions that decide everything

### Operator
Any person operating a website or online service who **collects or maintains
personal information from or about the users of or visitors to** such site or
service, or on whose behalf such information is collected or maintained, where
the site/service is operated for **commercial purposes** in interstate
commerce. Excludes nonprofit entities exempt from FTC Act §5.

Note "collects or maintains" and "from or about" — custody counts, not just
collection.

### Child
An individual under 13. (§312.2 definition — age 13 is the COPPA line, distinct
from CIPA's "minor" which is under 17.)

### Personal information (post-2025)
Individually identifiable information about an individual collected online,
including:

1. A first and last name
2. A home or other physical address including street name and city/town
3. Online contact information
4. A screen or user name functioning like online contact information
5. A telephone number
6. A government-issued identifier (SSN, state ID, birth certificate, passport)
7. A **persistent identifier** that can recognize a user over time and across
   sites — including a customer number in a cookie, an **IP address**, a
   processor or device serial number, or unique device identifier
8. A photograph, video, or audio file containing a child's image or voice
9. Geolocation sufficient to identify street name and city/town
10. A **biometric identifier** usable for automated/semi-automated recognition
    (fingerprints, retina/iris patterns, genetic data, voiceprints, gait,
    facial templates, faceprints)
11. Information concerning the child or the child's parents collected online
    from the child and **combined** with one of the above identifiers

Items 6 and 10 were added by the 2025 amendments. Item 11 is a combination
rule: innocuous data becomes PI when tied to an identifier.

### Online contact information
An email address or substantially similar identifier permitting direct contact,
including IM IDs, VOIP IDs, video chat IDs, and — post-2025 — a **mobile
telephone number**, provided the operator uses it only to send text messages
to a parent in connection with obtaining parental consent.

### Website or online service directed to children
A commercial website or online service, **or portion thereof**, that is
targeted to children. Determined by considering:

- subject matter; visual content; use of animated characters or child-oriented
  activities and incentives; music or other audio content; age of models;
  presence of child celebrities or celebrities who appeal to children;
  language or other characteristics; whether advertising promoting or appearing
  on the service is directed to children; and
- competent and reliable empirical evidence about **audience composition**,
  plus evidence of **intended audience** — marketing/promotional materials or
  plans, representations to consumers or third parties, user and third-party
  reviews, and the age of users on similar services.

Sub-rules:

- **(2)** Deemed directed to children when the operator has actual knowledge
  it is collecting PI directly from users of another child-directed service.
- **(3)** A **mixed audience** service is not deemed child-directed with regard
  to any visitor **not identified as under 13**.
- **(4)** Not child-directed merely because it links to one.

The 2025 amendments added the examples of audience/intended-audience evidence
and aligned paragraph (3) with the new mixed-audience definition.

### Mixed audience website or online service (new in 2025)
Directed to children under the criteria above, but **does not target children
 as its primary audience**, and does not collect PI from any visitor except for
the limited §312.5(c) purposes **before** collecting age information (or using
another means reasonably calculated, given available technology, to determine
whether the visitor is a child).

Any age determination must be done in a **neutral manner** that does not
default to a set age or encourage visitors to falsify their age.

### Verifiable parental consent / "obtaining verifiable consent"
Making any reasonable effort (taking into consideration available technology)
to ensure that before PI is collected from a child, a parent (i) receives
notice of the operator's collection, use, and disclosure practices and
(ii) authorizes the collection, use, and/or disclosure.

### Third party
Any person who is not (1) an operator with respect to the collection or
maintenance of PI on the service, or (2) a person providing support for the
**internal operations** of the service who does not use or disclose protected
information for any other purpose.

### Support for the internal operations
Activities necessary to: maintain/analyze functioning; perform network
communications; authenticate users or personalize content; serve contextual
advertising or cap ad frequency; protect security or integrity of the user,
website, or service; ensure legal/regulatory compliance; or fulfill a child's
request as permitted by §312.5(c)(3) and (4).

**Critical limitation:** except as specifically permitted, information
collected for those activities **cannot be used or disclosed to contact a
specific individual** — including through behavioral advertising — to amass a
profile on a specific individual, or for any other purpose. The 2025 amendments
clarified that such information *may* be used or disclosed to carry out the
enumerated activities themselves.

## §312.5 — consent exceptions (the ones that matter for school tools)

Verifiable parental consent is not required where:

- **(c)(6)** Collecting a child's name and online contact information to:
  (i) protect security or integrity of the service; (ii) take precautions
  against liability; (iii) respond to judicial process; or (iv) where
  permitted by law, provide information to law enforcement or for a public
  safety investigation — **and where the information is not used for any other
  purpose.**
- **(c)(7)** Collecting a **persistent identifier and no other personal
  information**, used solely to support internal operations. Requires notice
  under §312.4(d)(3). This is the exception analytics claims usually rest on.
- **(c)(8)** A persistent identifier and no other PI, from a user who
  affirmatively interacts and whose prior registration indicates they are not
  a child. No notice obligation at all.
- **(c)(9)** An audio file containing a child's voice and no other PI, used to
  respond to the child's specific request, not otherwise used or disclosed,
  and deleted immediately after responding. No direct notice; notice under
  §312.4(d) still required.

## The school-authorization position (guidance, not codified)

The FTC's ed-tech guidance states that **schools can consent on behalf of
parents** to the collection of student personal information, but **only if**
that information is used for a **school-authorized educational purpose** and
for **no other commercial purpose.**

Two conditions attach:

1. The ed tech service must **provide the school the COPPA-required notice** of
   its data collection and use practices.
2. The school or district should **give parents notice** of the websites and
   online services whose collection they have consented to on the parents'
   behalf.

Status: this rests on FTC guidance, **not** on codified rule text. See
`coppa-2025-amendments.md` — the FTC proposed codifying a school-authorization
exception in the 2024 NPRM and **declined to finalize it**.

## Analysis checklist — does COPPA bite?

Walk these in order and write the answer to each:

1. Is the entity an **operator** (commercial, in interstate commerce, collects
   or maintains PI from or about users/visitors)?
2. Is the service **directed to children** under the §312.2 criteria — subject
   matter, visual content, audience composition, intended audience?
3. If mixed audience: is there age screening before collection, done neutrally?
4. Does the operator have **actual knowledge** of collecting/maintaining PI
   **from** a child?
5. If yes to (2) or (4): is the data collected **from** the child, or **about**
   the child **from** an adult/school?
6. If collected from the child: does an exception apply — school
   authorization, §312.5(c)(7) persistent identifier for internal operations,
   or another?
7. Even if exempt from consent: §312.8 (security) and §312.10 (retention)
   still govern retention and safeguarding of any PI in custody.

## Anti-patterns

- **"We don't collect personal information."** Names are item (1) of the
  definition. Nearly always false for a platform holding a roster.
- **"The students are too young to use it, so COPPA can't apply."** Age makes
  the data *more* protected, not less. Argue the directed-to / actual-knowledge
  prongs instead.
- **"Analytics is fine, it's just aggregate."** Under §312.2(7) an IP address
  is a persistent identifier. The §312.5(c)(7) exception requires that it be
  **no other personal information** and used **solely** for internal
  operations — otherwise consent is back in play.
- **"The school consented, so we're covered."** School authorization requires
  the vendor to have given the school COPPA-required notice, and limits use to
  educational purpose with no commercial purpose.
